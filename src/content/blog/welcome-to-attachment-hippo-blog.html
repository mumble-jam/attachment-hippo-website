If you're like most people, you receive dozens — or even hundreds — of emails with attachments every week. Important invoices, contracts, photos, reports. They all pile up in your inbox, making it difficult to find what you need when you need it.

Why automate your Gmail attachments

Manual attachment saving is one of those tasks that feels small until you add it up. Open the email, click the attachment, download it, drag it to the right folder in Drive, rename it so you'll actually find it later. Repeat eight to twelve times a day, every day.

That's where Attachment Hippo comes in. It automatically saves your Gmail attachments to Google Drive, organizing them in folders based on rules you define — so you never have to manually download another file.

We built this because we were doing the manual saving ourselves. One PDF rule later, we haven't touched an attachment since.

Key benefits of using Attachment Hippo

Most users notice three changes within the first week of running their first rule.

Save time

No more downloading attachments one by one. Attachment Hippo runs automatically in the background while you focus on more important work.

Stay organized

All your attachments are automatically sorted into designated Google Drive folders. Set up rules based on sender, subject line, or file type to keep everything in the right place.

Never lose important files

With everything automatically backed up to Google Drive, you'll never accidentally lose track of a crucial document — or spend 20 minutes searching for an invoice you were sure you saved.

Access from anywhere

Since your files are in Google Drive, you can access them from any device, share them with team members, or collaborate on documents — no forwarding required.

Getting started is easy

The whole setup takes under two minutes. Here's how it works.

  1. Install Attachment Hippo from the Google Workspace Marketplace

    One click to add it to Gmail. No technical setup required.

  2. Connect your Google Drive

    Authorize the app to create folders and save files on your behalf.

  3. Set up your first rule

    Choose which attachments to watch for — by file type, sender, or subject — and where they should go in your Drive.

  4. Let it run automatically

    That's it. Matching attachments save themselves from this point on.
